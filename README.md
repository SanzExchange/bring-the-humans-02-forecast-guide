## What is a forecast bounty?

A forecast bounty asks you to predict a measurable outcome before a deadline, then prove the outcome happened. Examples of the shape:

- "This game will reach 10,000 players by Friday" — provable from a public counter.
- "The leaderboard leader on 1 December will be entry X" — provable from the leaderboard page.
- "This repo will pass 50 stars before the bounty closes" — provable from GitHub.

A good forecast has four parts:

1. **A claim** — one sentence, one number, one date. "X will happen by Y."
2. **A source of truth** — where anyone can check the result. A public page, an API, a leaderboard, a counter. If only you can see it, it is not proof.
3. **A deadline** — the exact time the claim is judged. After that, the result is frozen.
4. **Proof** — a screenshot, a link, or a record taken at or after the deadline showing the outcome.

If any of those four is missing, the forecast is not checkable and should not be entered.

## How to enter a forecast bounty

1. **Pick a bounty you can actually measure.** Read its requirements file end to end. If the outcome cannot be checked from a public source, skip it.
2. **Write your prediction down before the deadline.** State the claim, the number, the date, and the source of truth. A prediction written after the outcome is known is worthless.
3. **Build your entry.** For a guide-style bounty like this one, the entry is a public GitHub repository with a clear README. For a code bounty, the entry is working software. Either way, the entry must be yours and must be created after the bounty opened.
4. **Gather proof as you go.** Screenshot the source of truth at the start (your baseline) and again at the deadline (your result). Save links with dates. Proof collected late is hard to trust.
5. **Submit before the deadline.** Leave margin — do not submit in the last minutes. A late submission is a missed submission.
6. **Keep your proof.** After submitting, keep the screenshots and links. If a judge asks how you verified the result, you should be able to answer in one message.

## Your first forecast — a worked example

Suppose the claim is: "My referral link will bring 5 verified signups before 21 November 2026, 17:30 WIB."

- **Claim:** 5 verified signups via https://make.fun/r/hbzpx3ya.
- **Source of truth:** the make.fun leaderboard for bring-the-humans-02.
- **Deadline:** 21 November 2026, 17:30 WIB.
- **Baseline:** screenshot the leaderboard on the day you start (0 or your current count, with the date visible).
- **Result:** screenshot the leaderboard again after the deadline, showing the final count.
- **Verdict:** if the final count is 5 or more, the forecast was right. If it is 4, it was wrong — say so plainly. A forecaster who admits a miss is more credible than one who hides it.

Notice what this example does not do: it does not fake signups, buy signups, or count unverified clicks. Only verified human signups count, and faking them breaks the bounty rules. A forecast is only worth making if the result is honest.

## How forecast bounties are scored and paid

Each bounty states its own scoring. In general:

- **Correctness first.** A forecast that turns out right beats a confident one that turns out wrong.
- **Proof second.** Two identical predictions are separated by the quality of their evidence. Clear, dated, public proof wins.
- **Clarity third.** If a judge cannot understand your claim in one reading, they cannot score it highly. One claim per entry, one number, one date.
- **Payout.** Prizes are paid in USDC on Solana for this bounty. Winners are announced after the results date (6 December 2026 for bring-the-humans-02). Make sure the wallet and contact details on your account are correct before results day.

## How to track your forecast while it runs

A forecast you never check is a guess you forgot. Check it on a fixed rhythm — once a day at the same time — and write down what you see. A simple tracking table keeps you honest:

| Date | Count / value observed | Source checked | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 (baseline) | 0 verified signups | Leaderboard | Starting point, screenshot saved |
| Day 2 | … | Leaderboard | Any change? What drove it? |
| Deadline day | Final count | Leaderboard | Result screenshot saved |

Two rules for tracking: never edit an old row to make the past look better, and never skip a day because the number went the wrong way. The log is the proof that you watched the forecast honestly from start to finish. If two forecasters tie on verified signups, the earlier verified receipt wins the tie-break — so tracking early and submitting early both matter.

## Common mistakes (and how to avoid them)

- **Vague claims.** "This will do well" cannot be scored. Use a number and a date.
- **Private proof.** A screenshot of your own dashboard that nobody else can verify is weak evidence. Prefer public pages.
- **Moving the deadline.** Once you publish a forecast, the deadline is fixed. Do not quietly change it when the result goes against you.
- **One source, no backup.** Public pages go down. Take a screenshot and save the link — two forms of proof beat one.
- **Entering without reading the rules.** Every bounty has its own requirements file. Read it. The five minutes it takes are cheaper than a rejected entry.
- **Fake signups or fake numbers.** This is the fastest way to be disqualified from a referral bounty. Only real, verified humans count.
